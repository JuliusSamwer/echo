## 1. The paper claim (and how it has moved)

**Core thesis (stable across all pivots):** in a hierarchical latent world-model planner, the low level
already computes information the high level throws away — *how far execution falls short of the proposed
subgoal*. Make that residual flow **back up** the hierarchy and you get a closed loop: the high level
repairs or re-places subgoals mid-episode instead of committing open-loop to a plan. Positioned against
**HWM (Hierarchical World Models)**, whose interface is strictly top-down and whose known weakness is the
"reachability sweet spot" (subgoals that are invalid or too hard).

Three successive refinements, driven by results:

| Date | Version of the claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-07-09 | **v1 — reachability-triggered repair.** Trigger on `r_plan` (planner's own reachability residual); repair the subgoal by gradient descent in latent space. | Signal validated per-segment, but effect on episodes **modest / not significant** |
| 2026-07-28 | **v2 — two-pressure repair.** A subgoal is good iff *reachable* (bottom-up) AND *goal-directed* (top-down). HWM failures are reachable-but-**misplaced**, so reachability alone can't trigger on them. | Ran. **NULL** (see §5.4) |
| 2026-07-29 | **v3 — the signal is the contribution.** Every message in the loop was denominated in raw latent distance, which is nearly worthless for control. Learn a **temporal-distance head R(z,g)** on frozen JEPA latents (hindsight supervision, free) and make *that* the level interface: trigger, repair gradient, subgoal score. | **Current direction. Gate experiment not yet run.** |

**v3 pitch in one line:** *latent distance is not time-to-reach; a distilled head recovers the true metric
on frozen features, and trigger / repair / subgoal-placement all inherit the fix.*

  `g = l2_result.pred_obs[1]` → `l1_planner.reset_targets(g)`.

### 2.4 The residual (our instrument), defined exactly
A **segment** = one high-level replan interval (the low level chases one subgoal `g`).
Distance `d(a,b) = mean over features of (flatten(a) − flatten(b))²` — **exactly the low-level MPPI running
cost**, deliberately not a new metric.

- `r_plan  = d(ẑ_T, g)` where `ẑ_T = l1_result.pred_obs[-1]` — pre-execution, planner-derived.
- `r_exec  = d(z_achieved, g)` where `z_achieved = encoder(obs after executing the segment)` — post-execution.
- `r_plan_norm` = fraction of the gap left unclosed (coded, tested, **found useless**, §5.3).
- `goal_prog = d(z_cur, z_goal) − d(g, z_goal)` — top-down: does the subgoal advance toward the goal?

### 2.5 The repair operator
```
E(g) = α · d(ẑ_T(a*), g)  +  β · d(F_high(g, u₂..u_m), z_goal)
g ← g − η ∇_g E(g)          (n_inner Adam steps; defaults α=β=1, η=1e-2, n_inner=10)
```
- Term 1 is differentiable in `g` with `a*` **frozen** (Danskin).
- Term 2 should roll the frozen high-level predictor forward; **`F_high` is currently stubbed to identity**,
  so term 2 = β·d(g, z_goal) — a direct pull toward the encoded true goal. (The full macro-rollout was
  deferred: the predictor's conv/action-channel fusion fought it.)
- Frozen-parameter guards are asserted in code: only `g` ever receives gradient.

## 5. Results ledger (exact numbers)

### 5.1 M1 — reproduction (2026-07-09/10)
| | easy | medium | hard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Flat (L1 only), ours** | 0.975 | 0.700 (40 env) / 0.583 (6 env) | 0.500 |
| Flat, paper | 1.00 | 0.63 | 0.44 |
| **HWM (2-level), ours** | — | **1.00 (6/6)** | — |
| HWM, paper | — | 0.95 | 0.83 |

Reproduction is faithful; hierarchy ≫ flat on medium confirmed. Backed up in `zeus-p1/results/`.

### 5.2 M2 — is the residual real? (the de-risking gate)
On instrumented HWM medium, **n = 528 segments**:
- **Spearman ρ(r_plan, r_exec) = 0.602** (pre-registered threshold >0.3). ⭐ **PASS.**
  This is the single most important positive result: *the discarded planner residual is a genuine
  per-segment reachability proxy.* Biggest kill-risk retired.
- τ calibrated at 0.0765 from successful medium segments. Sanity-2 (does `r_plan` flag failed *episodes*)
  could not be computed — medium is a success ceiling, zero failures.

**Failure-regime trick (key enabler):** cut hard `n_steps` 500→150. HWM then fails 3/6 and runs ~3× faster.
This is the cheapest knob that produces failures to repair.

### 5.3 M3 — repair, n = 6 then n = 18 (hard, `n_steps=150`, paired)
n=6 three-way:

| | HWM | repair_always | repair_trigger (τ=0.10) |
|---|---|---|---|
| failures | 3/6 | 2/6 | 2/6 |
| repairs fired | — | 204 | **22 (~9× fewer)** |
| env1 (HWM fails, 3.65) | ❌ | ✅ 0.11 | ✅ 0.16 |
| env3 (HWM ok, 0.03) | ✅ | ❌ 0.53 **over-repaired** | ✅ 0.20 **spared** |
| env5 (HWM fails, 4.15) | ❌ | ✅ 0.22 | ❌ 4.87 (τ too high) |

Reading: repair does positive work; **the trigger earns its keep** — same benefit at ~9× less compute, and
its selectivity avoids the over-repair harm that always-repair inflicts. That is the thesis showing up.

n=18 scaled (paired, same episodes):
- HWM **7/18 = 0.39** [0.20, 0.61]; repair_always 6/18 = 0.33; repair_trigger 5/18 = 0.28 [0.12, 0.51],
  with 2.6× fewer repairs (236 vs 612).
- **Not significant:** McNemar p = 0.625 (rescued 3, broke 1). At n=18 one episode = 5.5 points, so the
  headline "39% → 28%" is a **2-episode swing**.

**Signal-separation diagnostic** (instrumented HWM hard, n=18, 7 fail / 11 succeed) — AUC(fail > succeed)
for `r_plan` / `r_exec` / `r_plan_norm` × {mean, max, final, sum}:
- **Best AUC ≈ 0.64**; most ≈ 0.55.
- `r_plan_norm` is **worse** than raw `r_plan` (max 0.40, final 0.46 — below chance). The
  "it's just a calibration problem" hypothesis is **empirically wrong**.
- `r_exec ≈ r_plan` — so it is not a stale-signal problem: **reachability itself barely predicts episode failure.**

**The reframe this forced:** failing subgoals are **reachable-but-misplaced** — the agent *can* reach them,
they just don't lead to the goal. Triggering repair on reachability was keying on the wrong quantity.

### 5.4 M3.5 — two-pressure repair (2026-07-28/29) — **NULL**
Step 2, trajectory-shape separability (oracle x,y distance-to-goal, n=18, 7 fail / 11 succ):

| metric | AUC(fail > succ) |
|---|---|
| `min_dist` | 0.974 (circular with the label — ignore) |
| `final_dist` | 0.961 (circular — ignore) |
| **`stall_frac`** | **0.922** ← usable, non-circular, live-computable |
| `progress_rate` | 0.234 (i.e. 0.766 inverted) |
| `total_closed` | 0.234 (0.766 inverted) |
| `late_backslide` | 0.623 |
| `last_third_closed` | 0.286 |
| *(reachability anchor)* | *0.64* |

So **goal-progress stall does separate failures (0.92) where reachability does not (0.64)** — the reframe
is validated *as a diagnostic*.

Step 3, running the repair with the progress trigger live (hard, n=18, paired vs the same HWM baseline):

| condition | failures | 95% CI | rescued | broke | McNemar p | repairs fired |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HWM (baseline) | 7/18 = 0.39 | [0.20, 0.61] | — | — | — | — |
| repair_alwaysB (ceiling, strong β) | 6/18 = 0.33 | [0.16, 0.56] | 2 | 1 | 1.000 | 612 |
| trig_progress | 7/18 = 0.39 | [0.20, 0.61] | 2 | 2 | 1.000 | 305 |
| trig_both | 7/18 = 0.39 | [0.20, 0.61] | 2 | 2 | 1.000 | 484 |

**Verdict: null.** Even the *ceiling* condition — aggressive goal-directed repair with effectively perfect
detection — nets **−1 episode**. This is the most important negative result in the project: it says the
mechanism is not merely under-triggered, it is **starved by the space it operates in**.

**Diagnosis:** the 0.922 separator is computed from *oracle (x,y) distance*. The model-derived version of
the same quantity, `d(z_cur, z_goal)` in visual JEPA latent space, separates at **≈ chance**. Every message
in the closed loop — trigger, operator terms, subgoal score — is denominated in raw latent distance, and
raw latent distance in this stack carries almost no control information. That 0.92-vs-chance gap is both
the diagnosis and (in v3) the paper's motivation figure.

## 6. Where the paper actually stands

**Established (defensible today):**
1. Faithful reproduction of both flat and hierarchical HWM on diverse maze (§5.1).
2. The residual HWM discards is a real per-segment reachability proxy: **ρ = 0.602** (§5.2).
3. Selective triggering beats always-repair at equal benefit and ~9× less compute, and avoids over-repair
   harm (§5.3, n=6 and n=18 both directionally).
4. A clean negative-plus-diagnosis: **per-segment reachability does not predict episode failure**
   (AUC 0.64), because failures are reachable-but-misplaced; and normalizing it does not help.
5. A quantified signal gap: **oracle goal-progress separates at 0.92, its latent-space counterpart at ~chance.**

**Not established:**
- Any statistically significant end-to-end success improvement. Best case is a ~2-episode swing at n=18,
  p ≈ 0.6.
- The compute-matched baseline (`hwm_replan` / `hwm_replan_every`) is **still stubbed** — `replan_high_fn`
  is a placeholder. Without it, no Stage-2 claim is publishable: a critic will say "you just spent more
  compute." **This is the single biggest hole.**
- The horizon-degradation sweep (the headline figure of the original story) has never been run on HWM_PLDM.
- Everything is n=18 in a budget-truncated regime (`n_steps=150`), which is slightly off the horizon axis
  the thesis wants; the truer harder regime (longer goals, block radii 17–24) is unused.

**Related prior work in this repo (toy maze, `zeus_maze/`, our own environment — pre-study, not the paper):**
- `closed_loop.py` with an **oracle doorway high level**: top-down open-loop 0.63/0.48/0.38 at L=2/3/4 vs
  closed-loop 0.95/0.95/0.87, all paired 95% CIs excluding zero, repairs/episode rising with L. This is the
  clean mechanism demonstration — but with an oracle high level and in our own toy maze.
- The rung-3 emergent-subgoal line (Danskin reachability energy, `rung3_*.py`) is parked; its Gate-0 result
  (reality-anchored energy is wall-faithful *and* smooth) is the ancestor of the residual used here.

## 7. Current direction (v3) and the decisive experiment

**Idea:** stop trying to make the *loop* smarter; make the *signal* correct. Learn
**R(z, g) = temporal distance** (steps-to-reach) on the **frozen** JEPA latents. Supervision is free and
hindsight-derived: any two states k steps apart on any offline trajectory are a labeled pair with answer k.
No rewards, no demos, no env interaction. A 2-layer MLP on frozen features; trains in hours.
R then becomes the universal level interface: trigger (replaces `r_plan`), repair gradient (differentiable
in g by construction), subgoal-quality score, optionally the low-level cost.

**Novelty position:** temporal-distance / quasimetric heads exist (QRL, contrastive RL, HIQL value-as-distance).
Nobody has used one as the **level interface in hierarchical latent MPC on frozen world models.**
Head borrowed, role ours.

**THE GATE EXPERIMENT — decides the fork, must be run first:**
train R on frozen latents from existing offline data, then evaluate it as a failure classifier on the exact
same diagnostic segments. Anchors: `r_plan` = 0.64, oracle = 0.92.

| Outcome | Meaning | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **R ≥ 0.85** | the info is in the latent; the metric was the only bug | **GREEN — the paper exists as v3** |
| **R 0.70–0.85** | partial recovery | mixed result, still writable |
| **R ≤ 0.70** | the frozen encoder discarded control information | a *more* provocative paper: "JEPA latents are not plannable"; geometry-retraining becomes paper one |

All three outcomes are publishable, which is why this is a fork rather than a gamble.

**Build status of the gate:** `pod_backup/reach_head.py` is written and self-tested locally — on synthetic
data it reports ρ = 0.65 for an informative latent and ρ = 0.02 for a random one, so the harness can
distinguish the fork. **Missing: the pod glue** that (a) encodes offline trajectories to frozen L1 latents
(`model.level1` → `backbone_output.encodings` / `obs_component`), (b) trains R and reports
`val_spearman` (the first gate number), and (c) a latent-dumping re-run of the diagnostic episodes to get
the failure-AUC. That glue is the immediate next build item.

## 8. Honest risks to hold

1. **Power.** n=18 cannot detect the effect sizes we are seeing. Sharpen the signal first, then scale to
   n=40 — n measures, it does not strengthen.
2. **Regime.** `n_steps=150` is a budget-timeout failure mode, not a horizon failure mode. The thesis is
   about horizon. Longer-goal generation is the honest fix and it is cheap (existing generator).
3. **Oracle leakage.** The 0.92 separator uses true (x,y). Any claim must run two tiers — oracle (proves the
   mechanism ceiling) and model-derived (proves it works from the world model alone). The gap between them
   is the mechanism-quality vs signal-quality decomposition, and it is itself a result.
4. **Circular metrics.** `min_dist` / `final_dist` are definitionally close to the failure label — never
   report them as separators.
5. **Spine discipline** (stated repeatedly by the user, and violated once): build the compute-matched
   baseline before any more mechanism variants. No Stage-2 number means anything without it.
6. **`F_high` = identity.** The top-down term is a direct pull to the encoded goal, not a macro-rollout.
   Defensible for now, but a reviewer will ask.

**Strategic frame (worth putting in the paper's intro):** the maze is not the paper — it is the mechanism
proof-of-concept, chosen because ground truth (true goal x,y) is cheap. If two-pressure/R-based repair gives
a big clean effect *here*, the claim becomes: "repair works when the signals are good; in harder visual
domains the residual risk is signal *construction*, not the mechanism." That converts an unbounded question
("does repair work?") into a tractable, transferable one ("how well can you estimate goal-progress and
reachability in domain X?").
